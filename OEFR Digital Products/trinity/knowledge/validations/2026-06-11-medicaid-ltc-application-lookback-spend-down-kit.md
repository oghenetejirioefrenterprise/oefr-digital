**Comment body (~215 words, adapt opener to OP's facts):**

> A few things that help when you're staring down the Medicaid lookback: (1) The lookback is 60 months from the application date (it's federal — 42 U.S.C. §1396p), and what it flags is transfers for less than fair market value — gifts, adding a child to a deed, selling the car to a relative for $1. Ordinary spending on the parent's own needs is not penalized. (2) "Spend-down" doesn't mean burning money: paying off their debts, medically necessary home modifications, prepaid funeral/burial contracts, and their own care costs are generally allowable uses. Keep receipts for everything — large undocumented cash withdrawals are what caseworkers question most. (3) If there's a spouse staying at home, federal spousal-impoverishment rules (the CSRA and MMMNA) protect a share of assets and income for them — the at-home spouse is not required to go broke first. The exact figures update annually and vary by state, so pull your state Medicaid site's current numbers. (4) Start assembling five years of bank and brokerage statements now — applications stall on missing statements more than anything else. (5) The moment anyone suggests gifting, trusts, annuities, or promissory-note strategies, that's elder-law attorney territory — penalty-period math done wrong can cost months of coverage.
