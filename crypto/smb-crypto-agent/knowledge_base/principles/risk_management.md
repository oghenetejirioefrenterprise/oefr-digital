# Risk Management

## Core principles

### Position sizing

- **Start with dollar risk, derive size**: Determine dollar risk first → identify stop level → calculate contracts/shares. Never use a fixed share/contract count across all trades. Formula: size = (dollar risk per trade) / (stop distance in price). Sources: 6_Must-Have_Rules_for_Trading_Psychology, The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **ATR-based sizing normalizes volatility**: Use ATR-based stop distance rather than a fixed-dollar stop. This normalizes risk across different volatility regimes automatically. Sources: The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader, 5_Practical_Tips_to_Master_Your_Trading_Psychology
- **Tiered sizing by setup grade**: A+ setups: risk 30–50% of daily stop. B setups: 10–15%. Exploratory/feeler: less than 5%. Only go full allocation on highest-conviction playbook trades. Sources: The_3_Trading_Problems_You_NEED_to_Solve, Brutally_Honest_Trading_Advice_to_My_Younger_Self
- **Scale up slowly**: Increase size 5–10% per week only. Never jump to full size after a single good day. Consistency of process must precede capital scaling. Sources: 5_Practical_Tips_to_Master_Your_Trading_Psychology, 7_Things_I_Wish_I_Knew_Before_Day_Trading
- **Size relative to skill, not aspiration**: Position size should reflect your current validated edge, not the account size you want to have. Sources: 7_Things_I_Wish_I_Knew_Before_Day_Trading, 6_Must-Have_Rules_for_Trading_Psychology

### Stop placement and loss limits

- **Define stop before entry, not after**: A stop set after the trade is emotional and arbitrary. Define the mechanical stop level — below the breakout bar low, flag low, or key support — before executing. Sources: An_insufficient_stop_loss_in_Valeant, The_6_Trading_Rules_I_Learned_From_1_Year_of_Trading, The_3_Trading_Problems_You_NEED_to_Solve
- **Never exceed your daily stop**: Daily stop = average of last 5 winning days. Never lose more than your average green day. Exceeding the stop once trains your brain that rules are negotiable — this is how accounts blow up. Sources: 5_Practical_Tips_to_Master_Your_Trading_Psychology, 7_Reasons_why_you_should_NEVER_exceed_your_stop
- **Three-strikes rule**: After 3 losses in the same setup on the same day, stop trading that setup for the session. This prevents the revenge-trading death spiral. Sources: The_6_Trading_Rules_I_Learned_From_1_Year_of_Trading, 5_Practical_Tips_to_Master_Your_Trading_Psychology
- **Never add to a loser without a new technical reason**: Each add to a position must be treated as a fresh trade with its own entry logic, stop, and risk allocation. Sources: The_3_Trading_Problems_You_NEED_to_Solve, 5_Keys_to_Make_More_Money

### Exit management and winner protection

- **Pay yourself — scale out in pieces**: Scale out as the trade moves in your favor. Do not try to hold for the entire move. Scaling out reduces anxiety, allows larger initial size, and produces more consistent results than all-or-nothing exits. Sources: The_exit_strategy_that_the_pros_are_taking, 8_Ideas_to_Help_Traders_Hold_Their_Winners
- **Concrete exit triggers**: Reasons to scale out — tape showing weakness, 2-min close below 9 EMA, indices weakening, VWAP lost after volatility settles, +1 standard deviation above VWAP hit. These are objective, not emotional. Sources: The_exit_strategy_that_the_pros_are_taking, Determining_Your_Reasons2sell_for_Your_Playbook
- **Trail stop to breakeven**: Once a trade has moved meaningfully in your favor, move stop to breakeven. Never let a winner become a meaningful loser. Sources: 8_Ideas_to_Help_Traders_Hold_Their_Winners, 7_Reasons_why_you_should_NEVER_exceed_your_stop
- **Distinguish "stop because invalidated" from "stop because scared"**: Know whether you are exiting because the technical thesis is broken or because of emotional discomfort. Big winners require holding through noise. Sources: 8_Ideas_to_Help_Traders_Hold_Their_Winners
- **Capture ratio target**: Target capturing ≥70% of the ideal move. Review capture ratio monthly to diagnose exits that are too early or too late. Sources: 5_Keys_to_Make_More_Money, The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Six objective exit signals**: (1) Long-term S/R level reached, (2) VWAP reclaimed against position, (3) intraday trend line break, (4) tape pattern changes (buying emerges on short), (5) stubborn bids at extreme (value buyers absorbing), (6) capitulation volume spike (2–3x average on reversal bar). Sources: How_to_Exit_Your_Winning_Trades_Properly

### Drawdown rules

- **Cut size immediately in drawdown**: Do not try to "trade your way out." Return to minimum size and focus purely on green days to rebuild confidence. Sources: 8_trading_lessons_from_a_painful_drawdown, 5_Practical_Tips_to_Master_Your_Trading_Psychology
- **Diagnose the drawdown**: Identify whether losses are from bad setups, bad execution, or bad market conditions. The fix is different in each case. Sources: 8_trading_lessons_from_a_painful_drawdown
- **Size down after consecutive losses**: After 2–3 consecutive losing days, reduce to 1/3 of normal size until a few green days are posted — even six-figure traders do this. Sources: 6_Must-Have_Rules_for_Trading_Psychology, 8_trading_lessons_from_a_painful_drawdown
- **Stop for 2 days after breaking your max loss rule**: One violation of the maximum loss rule requires a mandatory 2-day stop. This prevents the compounding of broken-rule behavior. Sources: 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose, 7_Reasons_why_you_should_NEVER_exceed_your_stop

### Psychology and decision quality

- **Pre-flight checklist**: Before each session, grade sleep, market context, energy, and diet on a 1-10 scale. This score determines your aggressiveness level for the day. Low scores = reduce size. Sources: 5_Practical_Tips_to_Master_Your_Trading_Psychology, 6_Must-Have_Rules_for_Trading_Psychology
- **Size down for life stress and fatigue**: Consecutive losses, sleep deprivation, and life stress all impair cognitive function. Reduce size mechanically when any of these are present. Sources: 5_Practical_Tips_to_Master_Your_Trading_Psychology, 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose
- **10-minute break after emotional trades**: Cognitive fatigue accumulates. Take a mandatory 10-minute break after any trade that generates strong emotion. Sources: 6_Must-Have_Rules_for_Trading_Psychology
- **Hide P&L during trading**: Watching P&L instead of price action shifts focus to outcome, not process. Hide the P&L display or size down until the behavior stops. Sources: 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose
- **Preserve capital above all else**: Small losses are recoverable; blown accounts are not. Every risk decision filters through capital preservation first. Sources: 7_Things_I_Wish_I_Knew_Before_Day_Trading, 3_Simple_Ideas_to_Dramatically_Improve_Your_Trading

### Tracking and accountability

- **Tag every trade**: Label each trade with setup type, grade (A/B/C), and outcome. Measure P&L by tag over 50-trade samples to identify where edge lives and where it degrades. Sources: The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Log every instance of your setup**: Systematic database tracking — entry, stop, ATR move, key variables, outcome. Derive price targets from historical averages of successful instances. Sources: The_exit_strategy_that_the_pros_are_taking, The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Win rate up, account down = wrong exit behavior**: If win rate is rising but account is declining, you are cutting winners short and letting losers run. Track average win vs. average loss, not just win rate. Sources: 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose

## Crypto adaptation notes

- **Daily stop calibrated to funding**: On Hyperliquid perps, overnight funding costs are a real P&L drain. Build funding payments into your daily stop calculation. A losing day + 0.05% funding is worse than it looks.
- **Liquidation replaces gap risk**: The equity trader's "overnight gap blow-up" analog is a liquidation cascade. Maintain margin buffer well above the liquidation price — treat the liquidation level as the hard stop, not the trade stop.
- **24/7 three-strikes clock**: The three-strikes rule resets daily. On 24/7 crypto, define your "session" explicitly (e.g., NY hours, or any rolling 8-hour window) so the rule has teeth.
- **Volatility regime matters more in crypto**: BTC ATR can triple during macro shocks. Size using current-period ATR, not historical averages. Revisit position size every morning.
- **Weekend liquidity reduces stop reliability**: On weekends, bid-ask spreads widen and stops can gap through. Either avoid weekend positions or widen stops and reduce size proportionally.
- **Funding rate as asymmetric risk signal**: Very high positive funding (>0.1%/8h) on a long position signals crowded longs — the same setup that creates explosive short squeezes also precedes violent liquidation cascades. Reduce exposure when funding is extreme in your direction.

## Common mistakes

- Setting a mental stop instead of a mechanical one; in fast crypto markets, mental stops are always missed.
- Adding to a losing position without a new technical setup to justify it — this compounds losses rather than averaging cost.
- Sizing the same for an exploratory trade and a high-conviction A+ setup; the tiered sizing system only works when applied.
- Trading through a drawdown at full size trying to "make it back" — this is the fastest path to account termination.
- Letting a winner reverse to a full loss because you refused to move the stop to breakeven after price moved in your favor.
- Ignoring fatigue and stress signals; trading impaired is equivalent to driving impaired.
- Watching P&L instead of price action, causing premature exits on winners and emotional holds on losers.
