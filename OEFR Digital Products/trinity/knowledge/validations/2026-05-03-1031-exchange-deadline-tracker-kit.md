### Post body (genuine value first; offer mentioned once at the end)

```
Most of the public 1031 content covers the SHAPE of the rules: 45 days to identify, 180 days to close, three identification-rule variants. The shape is well-covered by paid QI firm content marketing.

What gets less attention is the 4 specific places where the 45-day cliff actually disqualifies otherwise-prepared exchanges, and the kind of timestamp evidence Qualified Intermediaries say they want at year-end:

1. **The identification letter delivery proof.** Treas. Reg. 1.1031(k)-1(c)(4) requires a written identification "delivered" to the QI by midnight on day 45. "Delivered" is the operative word — not "sent." A Friday-night email at 11:48 PM to a QI's general-inbox is weaker evidence than a same-day timestamped email plus a same-day overnight-mailed signed paper original to the QI's office, both filed in a single dated folder. QIs who have defended audits suggest both methods, both timestamped, both retained in the year-of-sale return file. Buyers who relied on a single Friday-evening email without retention have lost on identification challenges where the QI's spam filter ate the message.

2. **Identification-rule misapplication when the buyer changes target properties mid-clock.** The 3-property rule, the 200%-rule, and the 95%-rule are not interchangeable. If the buyer identified five properties on day 30 thinking the 200%-rule covers them, the buyer must verify on day 30 (not day 45) that the aggregate fair market value of those five properties does not exceed 200% of the relinquished property's sale price. If the buyer wants to change one identified property to a different property on day 40, the change is governed by the same identification rules — partial replacement is not free. Most public free calculators just count days; they do not reconcile the rule-variant against the buyer's actual identification list.

3. **Constructive-receipt at QI funds-handoff.** A 1031 exchange is disqualified if the buyer takes "actual or constructive receipt" of the relinquished-property sale proceeds. The QI must hold the funds. If the closing agent inadvertently wires proceeds to the buyer's operating account on closing day instead of the QI's escrow account, the exchange is dead on day 1 — not day 45. This is rare but recoverable with same-day-of-closing evidence (wire-transfer instructions signed and dated before closing, copies in the closing-binder, QI confirmation receipt). It is unrecoverable without that paper trail.

4. **Boot reconciliation on Form 8824.** Cash boot (buyer received cash at closing), mortgage boot (replacement-property debt is less than relinquished-property debt), and net-debt-relief boot all affect taxable capital-gains recognition at year-of-sale return time. Form 8824 line 15 is the boot recognition line. Many investors who think they did a "fully deferred" exchange pick up some recognition on line 15 because of unanalyzed mortgage-relief boot. The 8824 walkthrough requires reconciling the buyer's actual mortgage delta against the IRS's mortgage-boot computation.

A useful starting checklist for buyers preparing for closing day:

- A countdown calendar anchored to the relinquished-property closing date, not a generic 45-day-from-today calculator.
- A copy of the QI engagement letter on file before closing.
- The identification-rule variant the buyer plans to use (3-property / 200% / 95%) decided BEFORE day 1, not day 30.
```
