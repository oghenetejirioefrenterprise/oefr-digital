## Core principles

### Building and validating edge

- **Edge first, size second**: Identify specific setups with positive EV and understand the why behind the edge before adding size. Structural edge (access, speed, information, analytics) matters as much as pattern edge. Sources: Brutally_Honest_Trading_Advice_to_My_Younger_Self, 4_Techniques_that_Create_Winning_Trading_Strategies
- **Backtesting requirement**: Backtest then forward-test every new setup. Require a minimum of 32 occurrences for statistical significance before trading with real size. Sources: 4_Techniques_that_Create_Winning_Trading_Strategies
- **Separate edge-building from profit-seeking**: The two goals require different risk postures. Edge-building requires small size and patience; profit-seeking requires playbook discipline. Mixing them confuses both. Sources: The_3_Trading_Problems_You_NEED_to_Solve, Bad_trader_habits_that_you_must_fix
- **Build from strengths**: Identify your one best setup and scale it. Slowly eliminate mediocre setups that dilute average trade quality. Do not try to be good at everything simultaneously. Sources: Five_Things_Profitable_Traders_Do_FULL_WEBINAR, Brutally_Honest_Trading_Advice_to_My_Younger_Self

### Specialization and playbook

- **Specialize in 1-2 setups**: Each profitable trader has 1–2 setups they repeat consistently. Refusing to trade outside the playbook is the core discipline that separates professionals from amateurs. Sources: The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader, A_Case_Study_of_8_Successful_Traders
- **Name the setup or skip it**: If you cannot name the setup, you are not trading your playbook — skip the trade. Playbook-based decisions eliminate real-time indecision and prevent impulse trades. Sources: Five_Things_Profitable_Traders_Do_FULL_WEBINAR, 6_Must-Have_Rules_for_Trading_Psychology
- **Grade setups A/B/C with 20+ examples each**: Identify the best and worst versions of each setup. A-grade setups hit you between the eyes — no convincing required. Measure P&L by grade over 50-trade samples. Sources: Brutally_Honest_Trading_Advice_to_My_Younger_Self, The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Strategy hopping is a failure mode**: Confusing market conditions with strategy failure leads to constant setup switching. Learn multiple strategies for different regimes, then stick to the one appropriate for current conditions. Sources: 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose

### Daily routine and preparation

- **Consistent daily routine creates best-day capacity**: Best trading days come from preparation built during quiet periods. The routine — pre-market research, game plan, setup scanning, journaling, review — is the job. Sources: 5_Practical_Tips_to_Master_Your_Trading_Psychology, 6_Career-Making_Lessons_from_the_Gold_Silver_Trade
- **Pre-flight checklist before every session**: Grade sleep, market context, energy, and diet 1–10. This score determines your aggressiveness level. Low score = reduce size; very low score = observation only. Sources: 5_Practical_Tips_to_Master_Your_Trading_Psychology, 6_Must-Have_Rules_for_Trading_Psychology
- **Solve problems same day**: When a pattern of losing behavior is identified, document it and create a solution before the next session. Never leave a problem without a fix. Sources: 5_Things_Discussed_Inside_a_Prop_Firm_Meeting
- **Force one day off per week**: Inability to take a day off is compulsive behavior, not dedication. Rest is part of the performance infrastructure. Sources: 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose

### Psychology and behavioral discipline

- **Focus on growth, not P&L**: Profitable traders try to improve daily; losing traders chase the number. The mindset shift from "make money" to "improve process" is the inflection point in most traders' careers. Sources: Five_Things_Profitable_Traders_Do_FULL_WEBINAR, Bad_trader_habits_that_you_must_fix
- **Identify emotional states before they trigger bad trades**: Revenge trading, boredom, and FOMO from a missed trade are the three main triggers of off-playbook trading. Recognize the state and divert to research or a break instead. Sources: Every_Trader_Faces_THIS_Issue, 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose
- **Overtrading is the most common self-sabotage**: Most PnL comes from a small number of trades. Adding trades on bad days accelerates losses. Set a maximum trades-per-day limit equal to your best days — not more. Sources: 3_Simple_Ideas_to_Dramatically_Improve_Your_Trading, 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose
- **Treat losses as learning, not failure**: Every loss requires a post-mortem and a concrete fix. The mindset that the lesson compounds is what keeps traders engaged through drawdowns. Sources: 6_Career-Making_Lessons_from_the_Gold_Silver_Trade, 6_Must-Have_Rules_for_Trading_Psychology
- **Life balance prevents forced trading**: Trading when emotionally or physically depleted is one of the most common causes of large losing days. Life balance is part of risk management. Sources: 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose

### Sizing development

- **Consistency before scale**: Demonstrate $100/day consistently for 20 days before scaling risk. Consistency of process must be proven before size is added. Sources: Brutally_Honest_Trading_Advice_to_My_Younger_Self, 7_Things_I_Wish_I_Knew_Before_Day_Trading
- **Scalp to build the floor**: Double down on scalping to raise the P&L floor before swinging for home runs. Scalping builds consistency; swing trades add upside after the floor is established. Sources: Senior_Trader_Presents_10_Essential_Adjustments
- **Test new setups at minimum size**: Grow any new setup with 1 share or 1 contract until it has 32+ occurrences of validated edge. Scaling a setup before it is validated destroys expectancy data. Sources: The_3_Trading_Problems_You_NEED_to_Solve

### Collaboration and environment

- **Collaboration surfaces missed setups**: Reviewing trades with peers catches setups, mistakes, and patterns that solo review misses. Daily trade sharing — even with a small group — accelerates development. Sources: A_Live_Trading_Event_Sydney, A_Case_Study_of_8_Successful_Traders
- **Match your trade type to your talent**: Deep thinkers with fundamental edge should use rollover patterns and swing entries — not momentum scalps. Understanding your talent edge prevents fighting your own cognitive style. Sources: How_to_make_trades_that_fit_your_unique_talent
- **Send daily reviews for accountability**: Send daily trade reviews to a peer or mentor even on small accounts. External accountability maintains the review discipline when motivation is low. Sources: Every_Trader_Faces_THIS_Issue

### Tracking and measurement

- **Know your statistics**: Win rate, average win/loss, best setup type, worst setup type, best time of day — these numbers define your edge. Traders who cannot cite these numbers are not managing their trading as a business. Sources: An_Interview_With_Mike_Bellafiore_of_SMB, Brutally_Honest_Trading_Advice_to_My_Younger_Self
- **Win rate up, account down = exit problem**: If win rate is rising but capital is declining, you are cutting winners short and letting losers run. Fix exits before adding size. Sources: 7_red_flags_you_are_about_to_lose
- **Journaling is the cure for drawdowns**: Every drawdown can be traced back to a period where journaling stopped. Track every trade with a reason for entry and a reason for exit. Sources: 8_trading_lessons_from_a_painful_drawdown, 7_Things_I_Wish_I_Knew_Before_Day_Trading

## Crypto adaptation notes

- **24/7 market demands explicit "session" definitions**: Without a market close, you must define your trading session, review window, and off-time deliberately. Without structure, 24/7 availability becomes a psychological hazard.
- **Funding rate is a P&L accountability variable**: Include funding paid/received in daily P&L review. Consistently paying high funding on losing longs reveals both setup quality and position management problems.
- **No tape in crypto — use order book and liquidation data**: The equity trader's tape-reading skill translates to reading Hyperliquid's order book depth, large liquidation events, and open interest shifts. Develop these as core skills.
- **Drawdown management principles apply 24/7**: The same drawdown rules — cut size immediately, diagnose source, return to basics — apply in crypto. The 24/7 nature means drawdowns can compound over weekends without the natural reset of a closed market.
- **Overtrading risk is higher in crypto**: 24/7 markets with high volatility produce constant stimulation. The maximum-trades-per-day rule is especially critical; set it and enforce it mechanically.
- **Collaboration in crypto**: The equivalent of a prop desk peer is a curated Discord/Telegram group with credentialed traders. Quality of collaboration matters more than quantity.

## Common mistakes

- Building too many setups simultaneously before any single one is statistically validated.
- Scaling size before demonstrating consistent process — this creates large losses before real edge is established.
- Confusing a string of losing days caused by market regime change with personal skill decay, then abandoning valid setups.
- Not maintaining a pre-flight checklist and trading at full size on days when physical or emotional state is degraded.
- Treating the daily review as optional — the review is where the compounding of lessons happens; skipping it means repeating the same mistakes.
- Watching P&L during the session instead of focusing on whether the current setup is still valid.
- Failing to force a day off, leading to chronic fatigue that degrades decision quality over weeks without a clear single cause.
