**Comment body (~210 words — adapt the opener to OP's specifics, keep the substance):**

> Two things people in this situation often don't hear early enough:
>
> 1) **Request your complete claim file before you do anything else.** If this is a group LTD policy through an employer, federal claims-procedure rules (ERISA, 29 CFR 2560.503-1) require the insurer to give you a copy of everything they relied on — the doctors' reviews, internal notes, surveillance summaries, all of it — free, on request. You can't effectively respond to a denial you haven't read. Send the request in writing and keep proof.
>
> 2) **The appeal stage IS the case.** You get at least 180 days from the denial to appeal (check your denial letter — that date controls). The critical part almost nobody tells claimants: if it ever goes to court, the judge usually only looks at the administrative record — what was in front of the insurer when it decided your appeal. Anything you don't submit during the appeal (updated treating-doctor statements, test results, a symptom/function log) often can't be added later. So don't fire off a quick angry appeal letter in week one; use the window to get evidence in.
>
> If there's a lump-sum buyout offer or it's a final denial, that's the point where talking to an ERISA disability attorney (most work on contingency) genuinely makes sense. Not legal advice — just the procedural map.
