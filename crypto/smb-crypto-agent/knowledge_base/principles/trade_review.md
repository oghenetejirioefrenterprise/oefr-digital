# Trade Review

## Core principles

### Review framework and cadence

- **Daily review is non-negotiable**: Review every trade every day. Categorize each trade as playbook or experiment. The daily review builds the pattern-recognition that makes the next session better. Sources: 8_Essential_Trading_Lessons, 5_Things_Discussed_Inside_a_Prop_Firm_Meeting, 6_Keys_for_Trading_Earning_Season
- **11 AM review framework**: Structure the post-session review around five questions — (1) Did the setup fit the catalyst? (2) Did the trade fit the setup? (3) Evaluate execution (fight for price, tape reading, technology, exit logic). (4) What is the next step? (5) What to do better? Sources: 5_Things_Discussed_Inside_a_Prop_Firm_Meeting
- **Review execution separate from P&L**: Judge the quality of the trade on execution — entry precision, stop adherence, exit logic, tape reading — not just whether it was profitable. A losing trade with perfect execution is a good trade. Sources: 5_Things_Discussed_Inside_a_Prop_Firm_Meeting, The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Monthly best-trade review**: Monthly, review your 5 best trades. Identify what to do bigger next time and what instruments (options, larger size) to use to express the thesis more fully. Sources: 6_Keys_for_Trading_Earning_Season, 5_Keys_to_Make_More_Money

### Edge identification and validation

- **Edge variables with predictive power**: Fresh news + elevated volume + volatile asset + multi-timeframe alignment. Identify the specific variables that predict your setups — not a fixed R:R ratio. R:R follows from finding real edge; it cannot substitute for it. Sources: 4_Techniques_that_Create_Winning_Trading_Strategies
- **Backtest before sizing**: Backtest then forward-test before risking real capital. Require minimum 32 occurrences for statistical significance. A++ backtested edge justifies 60% of daily stop; without it, use standard sizing. Sources: 4_Techniques_that_Create_Winning_Trading_Strategies, Backtesting_for_Trading_Success
- **Tag and track by setup type**: Label every trade with setup type and grade (A/B/C). Measure P&L by tag over 50-trade samples. Win rate by tag reveals where your actual edge lives and where it has degraded. Sources: The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Systematic database tracking**: Log every instance of a setup with outcome, ATR move, and key variables. Derive realistic price targets from historical averages of successful instances — not from wishful thinking. Sources: The_exit_strategy_that_the_pros_are_taking, The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Structural edge matters as much as pattern edge**: Access, speed, information quality, and analytics tools contribute to edge. Reviewing which of these you are underusing is part of the trade review process. Sources: 4_Techniques_that_Create_Winning_Trading_Strategies

### Exit review

- **Review every early exit**: Determine whether the exit was process-driven (objective signal triggered) or emotional (discomfort, fear of giving back gains). Emotional exits shrink winners systematically. Sources: 8_Ideas_to_Help_Traders_Hold_Their_Winners
- **Capture ratio analysis**: Measure actual captured move vs. the ideal move. Target ≥70%. Consistently below 50% signals systematic early-exit behavior that needs a rule change. Sources: 5_Keys_to_Make_More_Money, The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Distinguish "stopped because invalidated" from "stopped because scared"**: The first is disciplined. The second is a problem to fix. Big winners require holding through noise; a wider time-stop often solves this better than a tighter price-stop. Sources: 8_Ideas_to_Help_Traders_Hold_Their_Winners
- **Define custom exit signals per setup type**: Generic exit rules are less effective than setup-specific ones. Create explicit reasons to sell for each setup type in your playbook, both positive (sell into strength at target) and negative (stop out when pattern breaks). Sources: Determining_Your_Reasons2sell_for_Your_Playbook

### Setup quality review

- **A+ trades hit you between the eyes**: If you have to convince yourself a trade is good, it is not an A+ trade. Review how many of your recent trades required justification vs. jumped out as obvious. Sources: 6_Career-Making_Lessons_from_the_Gold_Silver_Trade
- **Lessons compound over time**: A missed $10K trade becomes the source of a captured $100K trade 6 months later when you have refined the setup. The review process is how lessons become repeatable edge. Sources: 6_Career-Making_Lessons_from_the_Gold_Silver_Trade
- **Specialization**: Profitable traders build 1–2 setups deeply and repeat them. Review how often you strayed from your core setups and what the P&L on those off-playbook trades was. Sources: A_Case_Study_of_8_Successful_Traders, The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Market context alignment**: A technically valid breakout in the wrong market context is a losing trade. Review whether each loss was from a bad pattern or a valid pattern in the wrong macro context. Sources: The_DDD_Short_Idea_SMB_Midday_Recap, A_Live_Trading_Event_Sydney

### Entry and execution review

- **Tape battle review**: The best entries come from watching the tape battle at a key level, not from a price target alone. Review whether you waited for tape confirmation or entered on hope. Sources: Catch_EASY_Trades_by_Avoiding_this_Tape_Reading_Mistake
- **RVOL confirmation review**: Entries without RVOL confirmation have lower win rates. Tag whether RVOL was above threshold at entry and measure outcomes by RVOL tier. Sources: The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader
- **Pre-trade checklist compliance**: Review whether you knew all required data points before entry — news catalyst, RVOL, VWAP position, ATR, sector ETF strength, float/short interest, entry/stop/target. If you didn't, the trade was not fully planned. Sources: Everything_You_NEED_to_Know_Before_Placing_a_Trade
- **Low-volume pullback adds**: Review whether your adds to winning trades came on declining volume (healthy consolidation signal) or rising volume (potential reversal). Sources: The_Daily_Report_Card_with_a_7_figure_trader

### Risk compliance review

- **Stop adherence audit**: Every session, audit whether all stops were respected. Even one stop violation must be investigated — it reveals which setup or market condition is weakening your discipline. Sources: 7_Reasons_why_you_should_NEVER_exceed_your_stop, An_insufficient_stop_loss_in_Valeant
- **Post-mortem on losses**: For every loss, define: (1) which rule was violated or which risk was underestimated, and (2) a concrete solution to prevent the same mistake. Never leave a loss without a documented fix. Sources: 6_Must-Have_Rules_for_Trading_Psychology, 8_trading_lessons_from_a_painful_drawdown

## Crypto adaptation notes

- **Funding cost is part of exit review**: On perpetual futures, every hour you hold costs or earns funding. Review whether exits were delayed to the point where funding cost eroded captured gains.
- **Liquidation cascade as exit trigger**: In the post-trade review, identify whether any trade was stopped by a liquidation cascade rather than your technical stop. If yes, the position was either too large or margin was too thin.
- **24/7 review cadence**: Without a market close, define a fixed daily review time (e.g., 5 PM ET) rather than tying review to session end. Review the prior 24 hours on the same schedule regardless of market activity.
- **Volatility regime in the review**: Crypto ATR can be 3–5x different between calm and volatile weeks. When reviewing execution, always note the volatility regime at time of trade — a wide stop in a high-ATR week may have been correct.
- **Open interest and funding context**: Add open interest direction and funding rate at time of entry to your trade log. These are the crypto analogs of TICK and ADD internals for trend day assessment.

## Common mistakes

- Reviewing outcomes (P&L) instead of execution quality — this fails to separate skill from luck.
- Not having a structured review framework and instead doing an ad-hoc post-mortem only after big losses.
- Skipping the monthly best-trade review, which is where setup-sizing decisions should be made.
- Accepting "the market was choppy" as an explanation without diagnosing whether the setup type was inappropriate for the day's regime.
- Reviewing entries thoroughly but not exits — most edge degradation comes from exit behavior, not entry selection.
- Failing to tag trades by setup type, making it impossible to identify which setups have real edge vs. noise.
- Not measuring capture ratio, resulting in systematic underperformance even on trades with correct directional calls.
