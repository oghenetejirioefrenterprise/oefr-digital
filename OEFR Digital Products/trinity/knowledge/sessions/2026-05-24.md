### [08:49] oefr_strategies
**cciephantom:** Here are ideas you can extract information from: https://youtu.be/iYFAG-uXlOk?si=aqYsv9jqoQHePWUC

**Trinity:** Patrick Dang's high-ticket B2B services playbook. Two layers here — the surface model and the buried mechanics.

**The surface model (not for us):** Sell $10K/month UGC creation services to CPG brands using AI tools like Arcades. Requires personal brand, sales calls, relationship-building. That's a consulting business, not a digital products business. Different animal.

**What IS extractable:**

**1. Signal-based targeting.** This is the sharpest idea in the video. Instead of blasting content broadly, he tells Claude to find *specific signals* that indicate a prospect is ready to buy — just raised funding, hiring for a role, running stale ads. We can apply this to our SSDI product. The signal equivalent: people posting in disability forums about upcoming ALJ hearings, asking about the 5-day rule, mentioning "pro se" representation. Those are people with a *ticking clock* and a $14 problem we solve.

**2. LinkedIn is an untapped lane.** We have creds automated, never used it for distribution. Disability advocacy communities exist on LinkedIn — disability rights professionals, SSDI claimants sharing stories, legal aid organizations. The SSDI blog post just went live. LinkedIn posts linking to it hit a different audience than Pinterest or X.

**3. Pain-reversal framing for product copy.** His sales call framework — ask about pain, list it all, reverse each into your solution — is exactly how the SSDI blog should read. "Most guides say submit on time. None tell you the 5 elements that make your INFORM letter legally valid." That's pain-reversal copy, and we already did it in the blog deploy this morning.

**What I'd act on:** LinkedIn distribution for the SSDI blog post. Zero-cost, creds already in `~/.profile`, audience-channel fit is plausible (disability advocacy is active on LinkedIn), and it doesn't compete with any active lane.
