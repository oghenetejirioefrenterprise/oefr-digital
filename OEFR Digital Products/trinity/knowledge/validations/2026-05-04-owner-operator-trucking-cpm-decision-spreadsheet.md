### Post body (genuine value first; offer mentioned once at the end)

```
Most public CPM content covers the SHAPE of the math: industry-average CPM around $2.26/mile per ATRI 2024, fixed costs + variable costs, divide by miles. The shape is well-covered.

What gets less attention is the 4 specific places where a load offer that "looked profitable" actually loses money once the math is run against THIS truck's specific CPM and IFTA exposure:

1. **Deadhead-mile dilution.** A load offer at $2.50/loaded-mile on a 500-loaded-mile run is the headline number. The actual rate-per-total-mile after a 200-mile deadhead from previous drop to this pickup is $2.50 × 500 / 700 = $1.79/total-mi. If your specific CPM is $2.30/mi (driven by your truck note + your insurance + your permits + your fuel + your maintenance), the load is $0.51/mi below breakeven × 700 total miles = $357 loss accepted. Public load-board math shows the loaded-mile number prominently and the deadhead exposure as a separate column the operator must reconcile.

2. **IFTA accrual blind-spot.** A load that runs OH → PA → NJ → CT crosses 4 states with different fuel-tax rates. The per-mile fuel-tax accrual for those states determines net IFTA tax owed at the next quarterly filing (Q2 2026 = 2026-07-31). A same-revenue load that runs entirely within a single low-tax state has lower IFTA exposure. Most rate-per-mile decisions ignore the IFTA lane-mix variable because IFTA settles 90 days later. The operator who tracks per-state miles AS MILES ARE DRIVEN files Q2 in 30 minutes; the operator who reconstructs from receipts at filing time spends 3–4 hours and risks an audit on bracketed miles.

3. **Lane profitability bias.** An operator accepts three $2.40/loaded-mi loads on the same lane because each individually looks acceptable against a $2.30 CPM. After deadhead and IFTA adjustment, the actual lane average is $2.05/total-mi — below CPM, but the operator does not see the lane bias because each load is evaluated in isolation. The quarterly lane-profitability tab (which lanes earn above-CPM after deadhead + IFTA) is where this gets caught.

4. **Schedule C category mapping at year-end.** The IRS Schedule C line items (Form 1040) include specific buckets: Line 9 Car and truck expenses, Line 13 Depreciation (Section 179 + Form 4562), Line 17 Legal and professional, Line 19 Pension and profit-sharing, Line 22 Supplies, Line 24a Travel, Line 24b Meals, Line 27a Other expenses (broker fees, factoring fees, ELD subscriptions, permits, IFTA tax owed). A year of un-categorized expense entries creates either tax-prep friction (your preparer charges more to recategorize) or missed deductions (per-diem, depreciation election, Section 179).
```
